My Journey into Data Science

To tell you how I came to study Data Science, we need to look back 10 years. At a conference for ceramic arts, I was inspired by a panel discussion on STEAM education. Problem-solving comes in many styles, and I wanted to train the next generation of creative thinkers. Fast forward to 2023, and I was looking for ways to meet my students’ needs for a different option as their third high school math credit. Many were not college-bound, so Algebra II didn’t make sense for them, but they also may not need the remedial Financial Algebra option that we offered. It was then that I found Stanford’s YouCubed Intro to Data Science curriculum. With the go-ahead from my principal, I enrolled in the week-long teacher course that gave an overview of the curriculum. Multiple times during my week in Palo Alto, I thought about how, if only data science courses had been offered when I was in undergrad, maybe I would have earned a different degree. A year and a half into teaching the intro to data science class, I was feeling the burnout from teaching and looking for a change.

I am still deeply committed to making a positive difference in the world, but how I want to achieve that goal has changed. My years of translating complex ideas into digestible engaging parts combined with the technical learning that I have done this year at Willamette have prepared me to unveil truths hidden in data and present it in an actionable way. We live in a time and place where data is constantly being collected. What we do with it matters. Whose story has been neglected? What are the needs in a community? How have the actions taken made a difference? Answering these questions is why I am a data scientist.
